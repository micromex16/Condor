Handicaps & Strokes ▾

Your handicap index represents your potential ability — roughly how many strokes above par you're capable of playing on a good day. In friendly games, handicaps are used to level the field between players of different skill levels.

Course Handicap — Before a round, your handicap index is converted to a course handicap based on the slope and rating of the course you're playing. Condor handles this automatically.

Stroke Allocation — Strokes are given on holes rated hardest to easiest (the handicap stroke index, printed on every scorecard). A player receiving 10 strokes gets one stroke on each of the 10 hardest holes.

Handicap Percentages by Format

  • Stroke play against the field — 100% of handicap difference.
  • Match play, one on one — 100% of the difference between the two players' course handicaps.
  • Nassau — same as match play. Full difference applied.
  • Wolf — strokes applied per hole based on that hole's stroke index and each player's course handicap.
  • Four-ball (best ball) — USGA recommends 90% of each player's full course handicap.
  • Scramble — varies by group agreement. Common formats use 25–35% of each player's handicap combined.

When in doubt, agree on the percentage before the first tee. Every disagreement about handicaps that has ever happened in golf happened because this conversation didn't.

Nassau ▾

The Nassau is the most played money game in golf. It is three bets in one.

The Three Bets

  • Front nine — won by the player or team with the lower score over holes 1–9.
  • Back nine — won by the player or team with the lower score over holes 10–18.
  • Overall — won by the player or team with the lower total score for all 18 holes.

Each bet is independent. You can win the front, lose the back, and lose overall. Or sweep all three. The unit value of each bet is agreed before the round — say $5 Nassau means $5 on the front, $5 on the back, $5 overall, for a maximum swing of $15 per person.

With handicaps — Strokes are applied hole by hole based on stroke index. The lower net score wins each hole. Whoever wins more holes wins that nine.

The Press

A press is a new side bet opened mid-round by the player or team that is losing.

If you are down 2 in a Nassau, you can press. This opens a new bet starting from that hole, running to the end of that nine. The original bet continues alongside it. You are now playing two bets simultaneously on the remaining holes of that nine.

Automatic press — Many groups play automatic presses — a press is triggered automatically whenever a player or team falls 2 down. Agree before the round whether presses are automatic or optional.

Pressing the press — If you fall 2 down on a press, you can press again. There is no limit to how many times you can press, which is why it is important to agree on rules before the round and not after someone has lost forty dollars.

Match Play ▾

Match play is hole by hole. The total score does not matter — only who wins each individual hole.

Winning a hole — The player with the lower score on a hole wins that hole. If scores are equal the hole is halved — neither player wins it.

The match score — Match play scoring is tracked as holes up or all square. If you win the first hole you are 1 Up. If your opponent wins the next you are All Square. The match ends when one player is more holes up than there are holes remaining — this is called being dormie, and winning from there closes out the match.

Example: If you are 3 Up with 2 holes to play your opponent cannot win. The match is over. You win 3&2 — three up with two to play.

With handicaps — The higher handicap player receives strokes on the designated holes. Only the net score on each hole matters.

Concessions — In match play you may concede a hole, a putt, or the entire match at any time. A conceded putt counts as holed. This is where the gimme lives. There are no gimmies in stroke play.

Wolf ▾

Wolf is a rotating game for exactly four players. It rewards bold decisions and punishes hesitation.

The rotation — Before the round, determine the order of play for the first four holes. This rotation repeats throughout the round. On each hole, one player is designated the Wolf. The Wolf always hits last.

How it works — After each player hits their tee shot, the Wolf must decide whether to take that player as a partner — before the next player hits. The Wolf cannot wait to see all tee shots before choosing.

If the Wolf selects a partner, it becomes Wolf + partner versus the other two. If the Wolf passes on all three players, the Wolf goes Lone Wolf — one against three.

Scoring

  • Wolf's team wins the hole — Wolf and partner each win one point from each opponent (two points each).
  • Opponents win — each opponent wins one point from the Wolf and partner.
  • Lone Wolf wins — the Wolf wins two points from each of the three opponents (six points total).
  • Lone Wolf loses — each opponent wins two points from the Wolf.
  • Halves result in no points changing hands.

Blind Wolf — Before any tee shots are hit, the Wolf may declare Blind Wolf — committing to go alone without seeing any tee shots. A Blind Wolf win doubles the point value of that hole. High risk, high reward.

Settling up — At the end of the round, total each player's points. Agree on a per-point value before you tee off.

Skins ▾

Skins is a per-hole game. Each hole has a value — the skin. The player with the lowest score on a hole wins the skin outright. If two or more players tie for the low score, the skin carries over to the next hole, increasing its value.

A skin that carries for six holes and finally drops is worth six skins. This is where dramatic moments come from.

With handicaps — Net scores are used. Strokes applied per the stroke index.

Settling up — Agree on a per-skin value before the round. At the end, total each player's skins won and settle the differences.

The best games are the ones where everyone agrees on the rules before the first tee shot. Condor tracks everything. You handle the handshake.

About
Condor
Golf & Wagering

Golf has always had a game within the game. The Nassau on the first tee. The Wolf howling on a par five. The skin that carries for six holes and finally drops on a downhill thirty-footer. These moments don't show up on any official scorecard — but they're often the ones you remember longest.

Condor was built for that game.

We're a scoring and wagering companion for competitive recreational golfers — the ones who play for something, who know the rules of Wolf without looking them up, who care about the number but care just as much about who owes who at the end of the round.

The name is intentional. A condor is four under par on a single hole. It almost never happens. But the people who play this game seriously know what it means — and they're exactly who we built this for.

Condor handles the math so you can handle the golf. Nassau splits, Wolf rotations, Skins carries, stroke play differentials — tracked cleanly, settled honestly, shared with your group.

No bloat. No gimmicks. Just the game.
